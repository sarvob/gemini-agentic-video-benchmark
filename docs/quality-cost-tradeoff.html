When better recall costs more

In five valid matched tests, Gemini 3.7 Flash with agentic video processing found three more brief events and produced stronger edit-decision classifications. It also used more tokens, cost more, took longer, scored lower on broad-moment retrieval and frozen evidence checks, and produced one additional invalid agentic attempt outside the matched comparison.

Scope guard. This is a small synthetic same-model comparison. No exact two-sided sign-flip result is below 0.05. The results do not prove general superiority, production ROI, or a Gemini-versus-PaperEdits difference.

The unmatched cost still belongs in the budget

The valid-pair table excludes solo-02 because its agentic response violated the frozen JSON contract and the static arm did not run. That is correct for paired quality comparisons. It is not correct for operational budgeting to erase the attempt: solo-02 consumed $0.075469 in paid-equivalent usage. Total v0.4 accounted spend was $0.660920, including all eleven requests and the invalid output.

What a buyer should decide before using agentic processing

  • Which quality dimension matters: brief-event recall, broad retrieval, evidence accuracy, edit decisions, or boundary precision?
  • What is the maximum acceptable cost and latency per usable output?
  • Will invalid outputs be repaired, retried, rejected, or billed to an attrition budget?
  • Does the workflow require a consistent gain across fixtures, or is a task-specific gain enough?

Do not turn the arithmetic into ROI

The extra matched cost divided by the three additional brief events is about $0.0254 per net additional event. That is a descriptive ratio, not a causal price or business return. The same runs also lost ground on other quality measures, and the sample contains only five valid pairs.
